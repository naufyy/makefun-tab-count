# Candidate tab counts

The field will cluster between about 20 and 150. Each line gives a number, the criterion it relies on, and the life it hints at.

## (a) Strikingly low but real (single digits)

- **1**: Crit 3 (far lower than anyone guesses) + 4 (anyone could have this right now) + smallest possible text. Someone who closes each tab when they finish with it, or just restarted after a crash.
- **2**: Crit 3/4. Work plus one distraction, like a tidy desk with a single coffee mug on it.
- **3**: Crit 3/5. Mail, calendar and the doc they are working in: the strict routine of someone who uses a tab manager.
- **4**: Crit 4/5. A small, deliberate set, like someone who pins exactly four tabs and never opens more.
- **6**: Crit 3/4. Feels like a student's morning: syllabus, LMS, mail, two readings and music.
- **7**: Crit 5. One tab per weekday plan, or a person who reads seven news sites every morning and then closes them.

## (b) Mid-range with a story feel

- **12**: Crit 5. An even dozen sounds like a recipe-and-shopping session in the middle of a cook.
- **24**: Crit 5. Reads like an hours-in-a-day habit, maybe someone who opens one tab per hour and never closes any.
- **31**: Crit 5. A month's worth: a person who keeps one tab per day of the month as a running journal.
- **52**: Crit 5. A weekly ritual: one tab saved every week for a year, a weekly reading list nobody cleared.
- **18**: Crit 4/5. Wedding planning or apartment hunting: a cluster of listings that is too specific to be random.
- **40**: Crit 4. A believable worker who has had a busy day, but it blends into the field (weak on crit 3).

## (c) High but believable for a tab hoarder (low hundreds)

- **312**: Crit 3/4. A real hoarder who has never closed a session, still plausible on a laptop with lots of RAM.
- **247**: Crit 3/4. A research binge; the odd tail makes it read as counted rather than chosen.
- **189**: Crit 4. High but ordinary for heavy users, so it barely stands out (weak crit 3).
- **500**: Crit 5. iPhone Safari caps at 500 tabs, so this hints at someone who hit the limit, but it reads as round and picked.
- **433**: Crit 3/4. Spread over several windows; someone who treats tabs as bookmarks.
- **999**: Crit 3. Reads like a counter that maxed out, but likely seen as a shock pick (crit 4 risk).

## (d) Odd/strange counts that read as a real quirk

- **0**: Crit 3. The browser is closed and they replied from an app. It is surprising, but the judge may not read it as a count of open tabs (crit 1 risk).
- **100**: Crit 5. Someone who closes everything once they reach 100 and is sitting exactly at the line, but the round number reads as chosen.
- **99**: Crit 5. Chrome on Android shows a smiley face instead of a number past 99, so this is someone who stops just short of that.
- **13**: Crit 5. A slightly superstitious, ordinary number that blends into the field.
- **64**: Crit 5. A programmer's power of two; hints at a dev, but feels picked.
- **8**: Crit 3/5. A low count with a quirk: someone who keeps exactly one tab per workspace.
